Power Dissipation

Understanding power consumption in digital circuits

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental causes of power dissipation in design.
  • Dynamic and static power dissipation.
  • Switching power dissipation, including load capacitance charging and discharging.
  • Describe short-circuit power dissipation and the conditions under which it occurs.
  • Identify various leakage currents.

🧠 Concept Overview

CMOS technology is popular because it has very low power consumption compared to other logic families.

Power dissipation in circuits is a crucial topic, because it directly affects the energy efficiency, heat generation and life of devices.

1. Power Dissipation

Dynamic Power Dissipation: This occurs when the circuit is switching states (0 ↔ 1).

Charging/Discharging Power (Switching Power)

  • Each time output changes, the load capacitance (CL) is charged or discharged.
  • Load capacitances including gate, wiring, and diffusion capacitances.
  • Every charging event consumes energy approximately equal to $\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{DD}}^{\mathbf{2}}$
  • The total switching power also depends on how often these switches happen (switching frequency).
  • Energy consumed per transition: $$E = C_{L} \cdot V_{DD}^{2}$$
  • Average switching power: $$P_{switching} = \alpha \cdot C_{L} \cdot V_{DD}^{2} \cdot f$$

Where:

  • CL = load capacitance, VDD= supply voltage, f = clock frequency
  • α = activity factor (probability of switching, 0 ≤ α ≤ 1)

Short-Circuit Power:

  • During the switching event, there is a short period when both the PMOS and NMOS transistors are partially ON.
  • Creating a direct current path between the supply voltage (VDD) and ground (GND).
  • This simultaneously conducting state causes current flow known as short-circuit current, contributing to power loss.
  • Short-circuit power: $$Psc \approx Isc \cdot VDD \cdot f$$

Static Power Dissipation:

when circuit is not switching, due to leakage currents.

  1. Subthreshold leakage current (Isub):
    • Even when a transistor is off, a small current can flow between source and drain below the threshold voltage due to carrier diffusion.
    • Flows when Vgs < Vth (MOSFET should be OFF, but a small current leaks).
    • Becomes worse with smaller transistor sizes (deep submicron).
  2. Gate oxide tunnelling leakage (Igate):
    • As the oxide layers become extremely thin, electrons can tunnel through the gate oxide, leading to leakage current.
  3. Reverse-biased diode leakage (Irev):
    • Reverse-biased diode junctions inside the transistor structure also contribute to leakage currents.
    • From source/drain junctions to substrate.

Total static leakage power: $$Pstatic = Ileak \cdot VDD$$

2. Total Power Dissipation

The total power consumption is the sum: $$Ptotal = Pswitching + Psc + Pstatic$$

3. Techniques to Reduce Power

  • Reduce VDD → biggest impact (since power ∝ VDD²).
  • Reduce CL → minimize interconnects, use smaller gates.
  • Reduce frequency f → lower switching activity.
  • Use clock gating → stop clock in idle circuits.
  • Use power gating → cut off supply to unused blocks.
  • Use multi-threshold transistors to reduce leakage.

Driving Factors: The increasing demand for mobile and wearable devices makes controlling power dissipation crucial for longer battery life and better performance.

Negative Consequences: High power consumption leads to excessive heat, reduced battery life, and potential device reliability problems.

Ultimate Goal: The main objective is to effectively balance dynamic switching losses and static leakage currents to create low-power, high-efficiency electronic devices.

1. What are the three main components of CMOS power dissipation?

  • Dynamic power, active power, and standby power
  • Resistive power, capacitive power, and inductive power
  • Switching power, short-circuit power, and static (leakage) power
  • Thermal power, radiant power, and conductive power

Answer: Switching power, short-circuit power, and static (leakage) power

These are the three primary components of power dissipation in CMOS circuits, representing different mechanisms of energy loss.

2. In the dynamic power formula, what does the symbol α represent?

  • Transistor gain factor
  • A constant for the manufacturing process
  • Absorption coefficient
  • Activity factor (the probability of the output switching)

Answer: Activity factor (the probability of the output switching)

The activity factor (α) represents how often a circuit element switches, which directly impacts dynamic power consumption.

3. What is the primary cause of switching power dissipation?

  • Simultaneous conduction of NMOS and PMOS transistors
  • Leakage current through the transistor gate
  • Charging and discharging of the load capacitance
  • Current flowing through resistive elements in the circuit

Answer: Charging and discharging of the load capacitance

Switching power is primarily caused by the energy required to charge and discharge capacitive loads during logic transitions.

4. What causes short-circuit power dissipation?

  • The circuit being in a completely idle state
  • A manufacturing defect causing a direct short to ground
  • Charging and discharging of the load capacitance
  • Both NMOS and PMOS transistors conducting simultaneously during an input transition

Answer: Both NMOS and PMOS transistors conducting simultaneously during an input transition

Short-circuit power occurs during the brief period when both transistors are partially on during switching, creating a direct path from VDD to GND.

5. What is static power dissipation?

  • Power consumed during the switching of transistors
  • Power dissipated due to leakage currents when the circuit is idle
  • Power consumed only when the output is in a static '1' state
  • Power lost due to the resistance of the interconnect wires

Answer: Power dissipated due to leakage currents when the circuit is idle

Static power is consumed even when the circuit is not switching, due to various leakage mechanisms in transistors.

6. Which of the following are three major types of leakage currents in CMOS?

  • Eddy current, saturation current, drain current
  • Subthreshold leakage, gate oxide leakage, junction leakage
  • Conduction current, convection current, displacement current
  • Short-circuit current, switching current, dynamic current

Answer: Subthreshold leakage, gate oxide leakage, junction leakage

These are the three primary leakage mechanisms in CMOS technology that contribute to static power consumption.

7. In older CMOS technologies (e.g., above 180nm), which type of power dissipation was dominant?

  • Static (leakage) power
  • Short-circuit power
  • Dynamic power
  • Thermal power

Answer: Dynamic power

In older CMOS technologies with larger feature sizes, dynamic power was the dominant component due to higher operating voltages and capacitances.

8. In modern deep-submicron technologies, which type of power dissipation has become the dominant concern?

  • Dynamic power
  • Static (leakage) power
  • Short-circuit power
  • Capacitive power

Answer: Static (leakage) power

As transistor sizes shrink in deep-submicron technologies, leakage currents increase significantly, making static power a major concern.

9. How does the operating frequency affect power dissipation?

  • Higher frequency increases static power but decreases dynamic power
  • Frequency has no significant impact on power dissipation
  • Higher frequency leads to higher dynamic power dissipation
  • Higher frequency leads to lower dynamic power dissipation

Answer: Higher frequency leads to higher dynamic power dissipation

Dynamic power is directly proportional to frequency, as more switching events occur per unit time at higher frequencies.

10. Which of the following is an effective method for reducing dynamic power?

  • Using power gating and high-threshold voltage devices
  • Increasing the supply voltage to improve performance
  • Reducing the supply voltage, clock frequency, and switching activity
  • Increasing the load capacitance to prevent signal noise

Answer: Reducing the supply voltage, clock frequency, and switching activity

These approaches directly reduce the factors that contribute to dynamic power consumption in the power equation.

11. Which of the following is a technique specifically used to reduce static (leakage) power?

  • Reducing the clock frequency
  • Using clock gating on idle circuit blocks
  • Using power gating or high-threshold voltage transistors
  • Minimizing the switching activity of the inputs

Answer: Using power gating or high-threshold voltage transistors

These techniques specifically target leakage currents, which are the source of static power consumption.

12. What is the technique of "clock gating" primarily used for?

  • Turning OFF the clock signal to unused blocks to save dynamic power
  • Turning OFF the main power supply to unused blocks to save static power
  • Increasing the clock speed to boost performance
  • Creating a more stable clock signal for the circuit

Answer: Turning OFF the clock signal to unused blocks to save dynamic power

Clock gating prevents unnecessary switching activity in idle circuit blocks, thereby reducing dynamic power consumption.

📝 Key Takeaways

Power Dissipation Mechanisms

Power dissipation in CMOS circuits occurs mainly through dynamic and static mechanisms.

Dynamic Power Sources

Dynamic power dissipation is caused by capacitive charging and discharging during switching, short-circuit currents when both transistors conduct momentarily, and glitches due to signal timing.

Static Power Sources

Static power dissipation results from leakage currents flowing even when the circuit is idle, including subthreshold, gate oxide, and junction leakages.

Dynamic Power Factors

Dynamic power depends on switching frequency, load capacitance, supply voltage, and circuit activity.

Technology Scaling Impact

Static power grows with technology scaling due to thinner gate oxides and smaller transistor dimensions.
