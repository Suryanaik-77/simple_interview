🧠 Knowledge Check

Q1: A coverpoint has 8 bins. After simulation, 6 are hit. What is the coverage percentage?

  • 60%
  • 75%
  • 87.5%
  • 100%

Coverage = (bins hit) / (total bins) = 6/8 = 75%.

Q2: What is the purpose of a <code>cross</code> in a covergroup?

  • To measure transitions between states
  • To measure all combinations of two or more coverpoints
  • To define illegal bins
  • To weight bins by frequency

cross creates a Cartesian product of the specified coverpoints, measuring how many of the combined value pairs were observed.

Q3: You define <code>bins b = {5}</code> and <code>ignore_bins ig = {5}</code>. Which takes priority?

  • bins b — explicit bins always win
  • ignore_bins ig — ignore/illegal bins override regular bins
  • The last declaration wins
  • Compile error — conflict is illegal

ignore_bins and illegal_bins take priority over regular bins. The value 5 will be excluded from coverage counting.

Q4: A coverpoint variable transitions 0→1→0→1. How many bins does <code>bins t = (0 => 1)</code> count?

  • 1
  • 2
  • 3
  • 4

The transition 0→1 occurs twice in the sequence. Each occurrence increments the bin hit count; a transition bin is considered 'covered' after the first hit.

Q5: When must <code>sample()</code> be called on a covergroup that uses explicit sampling?

  • Only at the end of simulation
  • Exactly at every clock edge
  • At the points in your test where you want coverage data captured
  • It is called automatically — explicit sampling is a no-op

When a covergroup uses explicit sample(), coverage is only recorded at the moments you invoke it. If you never call sample(), coverage stays at 0 regardless of signal values.
