🧭 What this challenge is

A variant of add_driver inside buggy_driver_ex2.sv that assigns vif.a, vif.b, vif.valid_in = 1, and then vif.valid_in = 0 back-to-back with no clock wait in between, before proceeding to item_done().

💡 The concept being tested

Driving a synchronous interface requires holding each value stable across a clock edge (@(posedge vif.clk)) so the DUT's registered logic can sample it; asserting and deasserting a control signal within the same simulation time step means the DUT never actually observes it as high.

🛠️ Debugging Guide
🔴 Symptom — what you see when you run it

The monitor observes zero valid_out pulses (or the self-check reports far fewer than 8 observed results), even though the sequence generated 8 items and the driver appears to loop through them.

🔎 How to investigate
  1. Trace add_dut's always_ff block: it only updates sum/valid_out on posedge intf.clk, sampling whatever valid_in/a/b are at that edge.
  2. In add_driver::run_phase, find the line commented // BUG: driving without clock synchronization and note there is no @(posedge vif.clk) between asserting and deasserting vif.valid_in.
  3. Compare this timing against the guided add_driver in driver_pkg.sv, which waits on the clock before/around driving so each value is stable at the sampling edge.
  4. Check what happens immediately after this block — is item_done() called before or after any clock wait, and does that explain why the sequencer moves on before the DUT ever sampled the beat?
🧭 Fix direction

Restructure the drive so vif.a, vif.b, and vif.valid_in <= 1 are set and then held across an @(posedge vif.clk) wait (using non-blocking assignments as in the guided example) before lowering valid_in and calling item_done(), ensuring each beat survives a real clock edge.

✅ How to confirm your fix

Re-run the lab and confirm the monitor/self-check reports 8 observed valid_out pulses matching the 8 driven transactions.
