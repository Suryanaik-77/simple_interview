🧭 What this lab is

The reference lab implements add_driver, a uvm_driver #(add_item) that pulls randomized operand pairs from add_sequence via the sequencer and drives them onto the add_if interface of a registered 8-bit adder DUT (add_dut), following the canonical get_next_item → drive → item_done protocol.

💡 The concept

The driver's job is split into two phases: build_phase, where it retrieves its handle to the DUT-facing interface, and run_phase, where it runs a forever loop implementing the TLM pull protocol against the sequencer. Each iteration calls seq_item_port.get_next_item(req) to block until the sequence supplies the next add_item, drives every field of that item onto the interface signals in a clock-synchronized way, and then calls item_done() to signal the sequencer that the item completed and the next one may be fetched. Reset handling matters too: the driver must idle its outputs and wait for vif.rst_n to deassert before entering the main loop, mirroring how a real DUT expects its inputs to behave out of reset.

⚙️ How it works

add_driver extends uvm_driver #(add_item) and registers with ` uvm_component_utils `. In build_phase, it calls uvm_config_db#(virtual add_if)::get(this, "", "vif", vif) and raises uvm_fatal("DRV", ...) ` if no interface was published — this is the standard defensive check every driver should have. In run_phase, it first drives vif.valid_in <= 1'b0 and waits on @(posedge vif.rst_n) so it never drives stimulus while the DUT (add_dut) is still in reset. It then enters a forever loop that calls seq_item_port.get_next_item(req) to obtain the next add_item from add_sequence, drives the item's a/b fields and valid_in onto add_if in sync with vif.clk, and finishes by calling item_done(). Meanwhile tb_top.sv generates the clock, instantiates add_if/add_dut, publishes the virtual interface with uvm_config_db#(virtual add_if)::set(null, "*", "vif", intf), and starts everything with run_test("driver_test")`.

🎯 What you'll learn
  • Implementing the get_next_item → drive pins → item_done handshake correctly
  • Retrieving a virtual interface safely via uvm_config_db#(virtual add_if)::get with a uvm_fatal guard
  • Synchronizing driven signals to the DUT clock instead of driving combinationally
  • Respecting reset (waiting on posedge vif.rst_n) before driving stimulus
⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Forgetting to call item_done(), which stalls the sequencer forever after the first item
  • Driving signals without waiting for a clock edge, so the DUT's registered logic never samples the intended value
