Signed Numbers

Representing Negative Numbers in Binary Systems

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why computers need special representations for signed numbers.
  • Explain and compare three main signed number representations.
  • Convert between decimal and signed binary formats.
  • Identify the range and limitations of each representation method.

Why Can't Computers Just Use Minus Signs?

Fundamental Reality: Digital circuits only understand two voltage levels - HIGH (1) and LOW (0). There's no physical "minus" symbol in hardware!

The Problem We're Solving

Think about how you represent -5 on paper. You write a minus sign. But in a computer's memory or register, everything is just 1s and 0s. How do we encode "negative" using only these two symbols?

Methods for Representing Signed Numbers

1. Sign-Magnitude Representation

The most intuitive approach: Use one bit for sign, the rest for magnitude.

Format: [Sign bit][Magnitude bits]

  • Sign bit: 0 = positive, 1 = negative
  • Magnitude bits: Regular binary representation of the absolute value

Example (4-bit representation):

+5 in Sign-Magnitude

Step 1: Sign is positive → Sign bit = 0

Step 2: Magnitude 5 in 3-bit binary = 101

Result: 0 101 = 0101

-5 in Sign-Magnitude

Step 1: Sign is negative → Sign bit = 1

Step 2: Magnitude 5 in 3-bit binary = 101

Result: 1 101 = 1101

Critical Limitation: Two representations of zero! +0 = 0000 and -0 = 1000. This causes problems in comparisons and arithmetic operations.

2. One's Complement Representation

Clever trick: Negative numbers are represented by flipping all bits of the positive equivalent.

How it works:

  1. Take the positive number in binary
  2. Flip every bit (1 becomes 0, 0 becomes 1)
  3. That's your negative representation!

Example (4-bit representation):

+5 in One's Complement

+5 in 4-bit binary = 0101

(same as positive representation)

-5 in One's Complement

Step 1: +5 = 0101

Step 2: Flip all bits: 0101 → 1010

Result: 1010 represents -5

Still problematic: Still has two zeros! +0 = 0000 and -0 = 1111. Arithmetic requires "end-around carry" which complicates hardware design.

3. Two's Complement Representation (The Winner!)

Modern standard: Used by all modern computers for integer arithmetic.

Why it's superior: Only one representation of zero, and arithmetic operations work without special cases!

How to convert to Two's Complement:

  1. Write the positive number in binary
  2. Flip all bits (One's Complement)
  3. Add 1 to the result

Example (4-bit representation):

+5 in Two's Complement

+5 in 4-bit binary = 0101

(same as positive representation)

-5 in Two's Complement

Step 1: +5 = 0101

Step 2: Flip bits: 0101 → 1010

Step 3: Add 1: 1010 + 1 = 1011

Result: 1011 represents -5

Quick Verification Trick:

In Two's Complement, the leftmost bit always tells you if the number is negative:

  • If leftmost bit = 0 → Positive or zero
  • If leftmost bit = 1 → Negative

Range of Integers Comparison

Numerical Ranges for n-bit Representations

Sign magnitude: -(2(n-1)-1) ... -0, +0 ... +(2(n-1)-1)

1's complement: -(2(n-1)-1) ... -0, +0 ... +(2(n-1)-1)

2's complement: -(2(n-1)) ... 0 ... 2(n-1) - 1

3-bit Representation Examples

Number Sign magnitude 1's complement 2's complement
+3 011 011 011
+2 010 010 010
+1 001 001 001
+0 000 000 000
-0 100 111 000
-1 101 110 111
-2 110 101 110
-3 111 100 101

Key Observations:

  • Sign-magnitude and 1's complement both have two representations of zero (+0 and -0)
  • 2's complement has only one zero representation
  • 2's complement has an asymmetric range: one more negative number than positive numbers
  • In 2's complement, -0 doesn't exist (it's represented as 000, same as +0)

❓ Check Your Understanding

1. What is the 8-bit Two's Complement representation of -42?

  • 00101010
  • 11010110
  • 11010101
  • 10101010

Correct Answer: 11010110

Step-by-step solution:

  1. +42 in 8-bit binary: 00101010
  2. Flip all bits: 11010101 (One's Complement)
  3. Add 1: 11010101 + 1 = 11010110
  4. Verification: Leftmost bit is 1, so it's negative ✓

2. Which of these is NOT an advantage of Two's Complement over Sign-Magnitude?

  • Only one representation of zero
  • Simpler addition/subtraction circuits
  • More intuitive for humans to read
  • Easier overflow detection

Correct Answer: More intuitive for humans to read

Explanation:

  • Two's Complement is actually less intuitive for humans because the binary pattern for a negative number doesn't obviously relate to its positive counterpart.
  • Sign-Magnitude is more intuitive because you can directly see the sign and magnitude separately.
  • The other options are genuine advantages of Two's Complement that make it better for computer hardware.

3. What is the range of representable numbers in 16-bit Two's Complement?

  • -32,767 to +32,767
  • -32,768 to +32,767
  • -65,535 to +65,535
  • 0 to 65,535

Correct Answer: -32,768 to +32,767

Explanation:

  • For n-bit Two's Complement: Range = -2(n-1) to +2(n-1)-1
  • For 16 bits: -215 to +215-1 = -32,768 to +32,767
  • Note the asymmetry: There's one more negative number than positive numbers.
  • Total unique values: 216 = 65,536 (including zero)

4. What decimal number does the 8-bit Two's Complement binary 10011010 represent?

  • -100
  • -98
  • -102
  • 154

Correct Answer: -102

Step-by-step solution:

  1. Leftmost bit is 1 → negative number
  2. Invert bits: 10011010 → 01100101
  3. Add 1: 01100101 + 1 = 01100110
  4. 01100110 in decimal = 102
  5. Therefore, original binary = -102

5. Which of the following is the correct 4-bit Sign-Magnitude representation of -6?

  • 0110
  • 1010
  • 1110
  • 1111

Correct Answer: 1110

Explanation:

  • In 4-bit Sign-Magnitude: 1 sign bit + 3 magnitude bits
  • Range is -7 to +7, so -6 is within range
  • Step 1: Magnitude 6 in 3-bit binary = 110
  • Step 2: Sign bit for negative = 1
  • Result: 1 110 = 1110
  • Verification: 1110 → sign bit 1 = negative, magnitude 110 = 6 → -6 ✓

6. In One's Complement, what is the 8-bit representation of -0?

  • 00000000
  • 10000000
  • 11111111
  • 01111111

Correct Answer: 11111111

Explanation:

  • In One's Complement, negative numbers are created by flipping all bits
  • +0 in 8-bit = 00000000
  • Flip all bits: 00000000 → 11111111
  • This is -0 in One's Complement
  • This illustrates one of the major drawbacks of One's Complement: two representations of zero!

7. How many unique values can be represented using 10-bit Two's Complement?

  • 1023
  • 1022
  • 1024
  • 1025

Correct Answer: 1024

Explanation:

  • For n-bit Two's Complement, total unique values = 2n
  • For 10 bits: 210 = 1024
  • Range would be: -512 to +511
  • Note: This includes zero and both positive and negative numbers
  • In contrast, Sign-Magnitude and One's Complement would have 1023 unique values (due to two zeros)

8. What is the largest positive number that can be represented in 6-bit Two's Complement?

  • 63
  • 32
  • 33
  • 31

Correct Answer: 31

Explanation:

  • Formula for n-bit Two's Complement max positive: 2(n-1) - 1
  • For 6 bits: 25 - 1 = 32 - 1 = 31
  • In binary: 011111
  • Note that 32 (100000) would be negative in Two's Complement (it's actually -32)
  • The smallest negative number is -32, largest positive is 31

9. What is the 8-bit One's Complement representation of -37?

  • 10100100
  • 11011011
  • 11011010
  • 00100101

Correct Answer: 11011010

Step-by-step solution:

  1. +37 in 8-bit binary: 00100101
  2. In One's Complement, flip all bits: 00100101 → 11011010
  3. That's it! No need to add 1 like in Two's Complement
  4. Verification: 11011010 represents -37 in One's Complement

10. Which signed number representation would give the binary pattern 1010 for the decimal number -2 in a 4-bit system?

  • Only Sign-Magnitude
  • Only One's Complement
  • Only Two's Complement
  • Both One's Complement and Two's Complement

Correct Answer: Only Sign-Magnitude

Verification:

  • Sign-Magnitude: -2 = 1 (negative sign) + 010 (binary 2) = 1010 ✓
  • One's Complement: +2 = 0010 → flip all bits → 1101 = -2 ✗
  • Two's Complement: +2 = 0010 → flip → 1101 → add 1 → 1110 = -2 ✗
  • From your table in the Range section: -2 is represented as 110 in Sign-Magnitude (3-bit), which extended to 4-bit would be 1010

📝Key Takeaways

Two's Complement is Standard

Modern computers universally use Two's Complement for signed integers due to its single zero representation and simpler arithmetic circuits.

Sign Bit Matters

The leftmost bit (MSB) indicates sign: 0 for positive/zero, 1 for negative in all signed representations.

Range Asymmetry

Two's Complement has an asymmetric range with one more negative number than positive numbers (e.g., -128 to +127 for 8-bit).

No Negative Zero

Two's Complement eliminates the problematic dual representation of zero that exists in Sign-Magnitude and One's Complement.
