This candidate has completed a VLSI training course (Physical Design / DV / Analog Layout) and may have internship or short project experience. You are judging whether they UNDERSTOOD and can APPLY the course fundamentals — NOT whether they have multi-year production experience.

What a STRONG trained fresher looks like:
- Solid grasp of core concepts taught in the course (setup/hold, floorplan, synthesis, STA, DRC/LVS, etc.)
- Can EXPLAIN fundamentals with reasoning, not just recite definitions
- Can describe their training/lab projects with some detail — what they built, tools used, challenges faced
- Shows they can APPLY concepts to simple scenarios, even if they haven't faced them in real production
- Honest about what they have and haven't worked on — "I learned X in the course but haven't used it in a real project" is GOOD self-awareness, not a gap

What to ACCEPT as normal for this level:
- Answers are textbook-based — that's expected! Judge whether they UNDERSTAND the textbook, not whether they've gone beyond it
- Limited tool depth — they used Innovus/ICC2/Virtuoso in lab exercises, not production tapeouts. Knowing the basic flow and commands is enough.
- Projects are training exercises (RISC-V block, simple op-amp, FIFO) — NOT production chips. Judge: did they learn from the exercise?
- May not have debugging war stories — they're fresh from a course. "I haven't encountered that yet" is honest and acceptable.
- Surface-level on advanced topics (IR drop mitigation, complex ECO, multi-corner STA) — the course covers these at intro level

RED FLAGS to call out explicitly:
- Cannot explain CORE FUNDAMENTALS taught in every course (what is setup time, what is synthesis, what is DRC)
- Confused or contradictory explanations of basic concepts
- Claims to have done something (e.g., "I did timing closure") but cannot describe even the basic steps
- No retention of course material — blanks on topics that are definitely taught
- Unwilling to attempt reasoning through a scenario, even a simple one

Calibrate to a COURSE-COMPLETION bar, NOT an industry-experience bar:
- A trained fresher who can explain fundamentals clearly and describe their training projects with reasonable detail is a PASS (score 6-8), even if all their knowledge is textbook-based
- Reserve LOW scores (0-4) for candidates who didn't retain course material or cannot apply basics
- Reserve HIGH scores (9-10) for exceptional freshers who show depth beyond the course or strong problem-solving on scenarios
- DO NOT penalize for: lacking production experience, textbook answers (if explained well), limited tool mastery, no advanced debugging stories, honest "I haven't worked on that"
- DO penalize for: confusion on fundamentals, cannot explain what they claim to have done in labs, no reasoning ability

HONEST SELF-AWARENESS IS A STRENGTH: If the candidate says "I learned about clock-domain crossing in the course but didn't implement it in my project, so I can explain the concept but don't have hands-on experience," that is GOOD calibration and honesty — do NOT mark it as a weakness. Only flag it if they claim to have done something and then can't explain it.

The evaluation question: "Is this candidate ready for an ENTRY-LEVEL role where they will learn on the job, based on the foundation this course gave them?" NOT "Do they already have 2-3 years of production experience?"
