📘 Theory and Background

What is Placement?

Placement is the stage in the ASIC physical design flow where every standard cell in the gate-level netlist is assigned a physical location on the die, within the boundaries established by the floorplan. It proceeds in three major sub-phases:

  • Coarse Placement (Global Placement): Cells are distributed across the floorplan based on connectivity and timing criticality. The objective is to minimize wire length while respecting congestion limits. Cells may overlap at this stage.
  • Legalization: Cells are snapped to the site grid, overlaps are removed, and all cells are aligned to valid placement rows without violating design rules. Cell positions change only minimally from the coarse placement solution.
  • Timing-Driven Optimization (place_opt): After legalization, ICC2 performs gate sizing, buffering, and limited logic restructuring to improve setup slack on critical paths. This is the most compute-intensive sub-phase and is what the ICC2 command place_opt invokes as an integrated flow covering all three sub-phases.

The quality of placement directly determines the achievable timing closure and routability in subsequent CTS and routing stages. A poor placement cannot be fully corrected by routing.

Why Setup-Only During Placement?

During placement, the clock tree does not yet exist — clocks are propagated using ideal (zero-skew) assumptions. Under ideal clock propagation, hold timing analysis produces meaningless results because hold violations depend on clock skew between launch and capture flip-flops, which is zero by assumption. Running hold optimization at this stage would:

  • Insert hold buffers on paths that may not need them once real clock skew is introduced at CTS.
  • Waste area and increase congestion by adding unnecessary buffers.
  • Slow down place_opt with analysis that gives no useful signal.

Hold violations are fixed in the post-CTS optimization stage (clock_opt -only_hold_time) when the real clock tree and its skew are both known. ICC2 enforces this discipline by allowing you to deactivate hold scenarios with set_scenario_status -active false before placement runs.

Key rule: Activate only *setup* scenarios before running place_opt. Reactivate hold scenarios after CTS.

IO Buffer Insertion and magnet_placement

IO ports are the interface between the design and the outside world. During placement, standard cells connected directly to ports may be placed far from the ports, creating long, undriven wires and slowing down the paths. The solution is to insert buffers directly at the ports before placement runs:

  • add_buffer: Inserts a specified buffer cell (e.g., BUFFD4BWP35P140) on every data port, giving the optimizer a concrete driver cell to work with. The -new_cell_names IO_buff argument names these instances for later reference.
  • magnet_placement: With -mark_legalize_only, this command marks the IO buffer cells as "magnets" — during legalization, ICC2 will pull these cells as close to their associated port as possible. Without this, the legalizer may move IO buffers far from the port.
  • set_dont_touch: Prevents place_opt from removing or replacing the IO buffers during timing optimization. Without this, the optimizer may delete them to improve timing on other paths, defeating their purpose.

Lib-Cell Dont-Use During Placement

Not all cells in the standard cell library should be used during placement optimization. Using inappropriate cells leads to power problems, congestion, or downstream flow failures. The set_lib_cell_purpose -exclude optimization command prevents ICC2 from inserting or upsizing these cell types during place_opt:

  • */CK* — Clock cells: Clock buffers and inverters are managed exclusively by the CTS engine. Inserting them during placement creates clock networks that conflict with the CTS solution.
  • */*D24*, */*D32*, */*D20*, */*D18* — Very high drive-strength cells: These cells have large widths and create severe local congestion when placed in tight areas. They are only permitted if routing analysis confirms they are necessary.
  • */DEL* — Delay cells: Pure delay cells are inserted only in specific scenarios (scan chain balancing, hold fixing). Allowing them during placement optimization produces false timing improvement that disappears post-routing.
  • */*D0* — Minimum-drive cells: Cells with drive strength D0 are the weakest in the library and typically insufficient for any meaningful fanout. Using them during optimization leads to immediate upsizing and wasted iterations.
  • */*LVT — Low-Vt cells: LVT cells have higher leakage power. During placement, the optimizer is not yet aware of final power budgets. Excluding LVT avoids power blowup; LVT cells are selectively permitted in post-route optimization if timing requires them.

Congestion-Driven Placement Settings

Congestion during placement is measured by the global routing congestion overflow — the ratio of routing demand to routing supply on each metal layer. If the placer creates too-dense clusters of cells, the router will not be able to complete all nets, producing an unroutable design. Key settings to control congestion:

  • place_opt.place.congestion_effort high: Tells place_opt to spend more CPU time spreading cells to reduce congestion, even at the cost of slightly worse initial timing.
  • place.coarse.congestion_layer_aware true: Enables per-layer congestion estimation during coarse placement, so that layers with heavy power stripes are accounted for and the placer avoids routing through them.
  • place.coarse.cong_restruct_effort high: Activates aggressive cell restructuring during coarse placement to break up congested clusters by re-clustering logic around multiple spreading centers.
  • place.coarse.max_density 0.7: Limits the local cell density to 70% of the available routing resources per tile. This leaves 30% headroom for routing and filler cells, reducing the risk of post-route DRC violations.

CCD — Why Disabled at Placement

Concurrent Clock and Data (CCD) optimization is an ICC2 technique that jointly optimizes clock tree topology and data path timing during a single pass. It is powerful but requires a nearly complete clock tree structure to make valid decisions. During placement, no clock tree exists — enabling CCD at this stage would cause ICC2 to make clock tree shape decisions based on synthetic estimates that will be discarded when the real CTS runs. The result is longer place_opt runtime with no quality benefit. CCD is enabled by default in the clock_opt command during the CTS stage, which is where it belongs.

Rule: Always set place_opt.flow.enable_ccd false before running place_opt. CCD is for CTS, not placement.

📝 Knowledge Assessment Quiz

Test your understanding of the placement concepts covered above. Click on your answer.

1. Why are hold scenarios deactivated before running place_opt?

  • Hold analysis requires a routed netlist — it cannot be computed on unplaced cells.
  • Hold violations are impossible to fix during placement because the optimizer has no buffers available.
  • The clock tree does not exist during placement, so ideal clock propagation produces zero skew. Hold optimization under zero skew inserts buffers that are unnecessary and harmful once real clock skew is introduced at CTS.
  • ICC2 always ignores hold scenarios during place_opt regardless of the active scenario status setting.
Correct! Hold violations are skew-dependent. Under ideal clock propagation (zero skew), ICC2 would compute hold slack as if the clock arrives simultaneously at all flip-flops, which is never true in silicon. Hold fixing at this stage inserts unnecessary buffers that waste area and increase congestion. The correct stage for hold fixing is post-CTS optimization when the real clock tree and its skew are both known.

2. What is the role of magnet_placement in the IO buffer insertion flow?

  • It inserts the IO buffer cells into the netlist at each port location.
  • It marks the IO buffer cells so that the legalizer pulls them as physically close to their associated port as possible, preventing the legalizer from moving them away from the port boundary.
  • It assigns a dont-touch attribute to all IO buffers so they cannot be removed during place_opt.
  • It routes a stub wire from each port pin to the closest IO buffer cell after legalization.
Correct! magnet_placement -mark_legalize_only marks the IO buffer instances as legalization magnets. During the legalization phase of placement, ICC2 will pull these cells toward their associated ports rather than placing them at arbitrary legal positions in the standard cell rows. This minimizes the wire length from each port to its first buffer, reducing input/output path delays.

3. What does the ICC2 command place_opt actually execute as an integrated flow?

  • Only global placement — legalize_placement and timing optimization must be run as separate commands afterward.
  • Only timing-driven optimization on a pre-placed netlist — the designer must run coarse placement manually before calling place_opt.
  • An integrated flow covering coarse (global) placement, legalization, and timing-driven optimization (gate sizing, buffering, limited logic restructuring) in a single command.
  • Clock tree synthesis followed by placement — CCD mode is always enabled during place_opt by default.
Correct! place_opt is ICC2's integrated placement command. It executes coarse placement to distribute cells based on timing and connectivity, legalizes the solution to remove overlaps and snap cells to the placement grid, and then performs timing-driven optimization including gate sizing, buffer insertion, and selective logic restructuring — all in one command call.

4. Why are LVT (Low-Vt) cells excluded from optimization during placement using set_lib_cell_purpose -exclude optimization?

  • LVT cells have slower timing than SVT cells and would worsen setup violations if used during placement.
  • LVT cells cannot be legally placed in standard cell rows and must be placed in special LVT regions defined during floorplanning.
  • LVT cells have higher leakage power. Allowing them during placement would cause the optimizer to use them freely throughout the design, blowing up static power before any power-timing tradeoff analysis has been done.
  • LVT cells are only available in the hold timing library corners and are incompatible with the setup-only scenario mode used during placement.
Correct! LVT cells are faster than SVT cells but consume significantly more leakage power due to their lower threshold voltage. Placing them freely during place_opt — before any power budget analysis — would cause the design to exceed its leakage power target. LVT cells are selectively re-enabled in post-route optimization only on paths that cannot close timing with SVT cells, after a power-aware timing analysis confirms the budget allows it.
