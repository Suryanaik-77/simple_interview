Why this order

Stage 1 · Synthesis

Everything in physical design starts here. You cannot place and route a design that has not been synthesized into a gate-level netlist, so Synthesis is the entry point for every other stage in this course.

Stage 2 · LEC

Immediately after synthesis, verify that the netlist is functionally identical to the RTL. Catching equivalence failures before committing to layout is far cheaper than finding them after tape-out. LEC is placed here because it is a natural synthesis checkpoint — but it is not a hard prerequisite for PnR. See the flexible paths below.

Stage 3 · PnR

The core of physical design. Modules are sequenced to mirror the actual PnR flow: floorplan before placement, placement before routing, routing before chip finish. The stage order within PnR is fixed; the entry point into the course is not.

Stage 4 · PV

Physical verification operates on a completed, routed layout — it has no meaning earlier in the flow. Calibre is used here because it is the industry sign-off standard, independent of which PnR tool produced the layout.

Stage 5 · STA

Full static timing analysis — especially multi-corner DMSA — requires real post-route parasitics from the finished layout. Running STA last means the timing numbers are accurate and the ECOs generated are directly implementable.
